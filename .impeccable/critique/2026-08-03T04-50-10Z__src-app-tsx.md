## Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment:** Moderately specific (3/5). The navy/teal clinical language, Spanish-Chile positioning, real dental-lab scenes, and case/order/payment vocabulary feel authored for Comelu. The structural sequence still feels like interchangeable SaaS—hero, pain carousel, audience cards, proof area, long waitlist form—and relies too often on “simple, moderna, amigable” instead of a singular case-level operating thesis.

**Deterministic scan:** Clean: `detect.mjs --json src/App.tsx` returned `[]` with exit code 0. It found no rule names, locations, or false positives. Browser automation was unavailable, so no live visual overlay or browser-console evidence could be collected.

## Overall Impression

The landing starts with credible, calm dental-lab specificity, but its most important trust signals are misaligned: a visibly unfinished placeholder and a polished dashboard mockup for an unshipped product. The largest opportunity is an honest, unforgettable story around one dental case—its files, production state, and payment context—rather than a generic promise of modern software.

## What's Working

- Domain grounding is unusually strong for an early landing: cases, production states, files, payments, and dental-lab imagery form a credible world.
- The navy/paper/teal system is coherent and restrained, with visible focus states and a `prefers-reduced-motion` path.
- The FAQ is forthright about the product being in development and avoids unsupported customers, metrics, or testimonials.

## Priority Issues

### [P0] Visible placeholder in the credibility section

**Why it matters:** “Placeholder credibilidad” plus image specs and asset instructions is a public-facing unfinished artifact. It directly undercuts the premium, precise positioning.

**Fix:** Remove that section until approved evidence or imagery exists. If it must remain, replace it with a finished, explicitly conceptual workflow—not internal production notes.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable polish`

### [P0] Hero visual overclaims product maturity

**Why it matters:** The dashboard/phone mock presents identifiable rows, people, dollar figures, and a working-looking product while the page says the software is being built. Prospects can reasonably infer live functionality.

**Fix:** Label a simplified interface explicitly as a concept in development and remove invented names/metrics/currency, or use an honest lab-context image while describing the future case journey.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable clarify`

### [P1] Broad, overloaded first fold

**Why it matters:** The hero asks visitors to process an eyebrow, H1, two paragraphs, four bullets, and two CTAs. Its language is too generic for a product with such specific source material.

**Fix:** Lead with one operational proposition—for example, “Cada caso, sus archivos, estado y cobro en el mismo lugar”—then keep two specific benefits and a single primary CTA.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable clarify`

### [P1] Auto-advancing carousel hides the core proof

**Why it matters:** Four problem stories rotate every eight seconds behind thin progress strips; people cannot pause, compare, or scan their relevance. This creates unnecessary cognitive load, especially on mobile.

**Fix:** Use four static, numbered problem cards, or add visible previous/next/pause controls and labeled tabs; default to manual navigation.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable distill`

### [P1] Waitlist form mixes lead capture with research

**Why it matters:** A busy lab owner reaches a long form with role, phone, team size, six interests, and a free-text request. That turns low-commitment signup into unpaid discovery work.

**Fix:** Make name, email, and role the initial action; collect research answers after success or in a clearly optional second step. Consolidate the privacy/contact reassurance into one plain statement.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable onboard`

## Persona Red Flags

**Daniela, owner of a 4–10 person dental lab:** The hero reads as a live tool despite the pre-launch status. Dollar figures and invented-looking names make the offer feel speculative rather than trustworthy. Near the CTA, she does not get a concise answer to what happens after signup or when she will hear from Comelu.

**Matías, independent laboratorista working from bench and phone:** The carousel advances while he is interrupted; its thin unlabeled controls are hard to operate or revisit. The long waitlist asks him to rank hypotheticals and write product feedback before he receives value.

**Fernanda, supervisor and first-time evaluator:** To assess fit, she must remember four serial pain stories, infer what is available today, then pick through six roles despite the page having a clear primary audience.

## Minor Observations

- The mobile-menu button remains labelled “Abrir menú” even when the menu is open; make the state label dynamic.
- The logo uses `href="#"`, causing a needless jump to the page top; use the real home route.
- Hover-lift on non-interactive audience articles suggests clickability.
- FAQ answers repeatedly begin with unqualified “Sí” for a future product; qualify intended scope where necessary.
- The exact 11-digit phone rule will reject valid non-Chilean or variable-length numbers.

## Questions to Consider

- What if the page demonstrated one honest case lifecycle instead of a dashboard mockup?
- What could a lab owner repeat after five seconds without the words “simple,” “moderno,” or “amigable”?
- Is this form primarily a waitlist conversion, or a research-recruitment tool—and should those be two separate commitments?
